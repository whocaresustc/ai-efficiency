Agents

Architectures, patterns, and the shift to agent-scale engineering. Last refreshed 2026-10-28.

What changed recently — Agent output is now outpacing the tooling built for humans: Anthropic reports internal code-merge volume went from ~2,500/day (May) to 30,000+/day (Sept), forcing a rebuild of core dev infra for agent scale. Claude Code shipped a "v2" built around running many agent sessions side-by-side with agents aware enough to spin off their own sub-sessions. Why agents are the center of gravity

Across the daily reports, "agent" is the single most recurring theme — ahead of any individual model. The field has moved from "LLM that answers" to "agent that does work over many steps," and the hard problems have shifted accordingly: orchestration, memory, self-awareness, and the infrastructure to absorb agent-scale output.

Multi-agent & sub-agents

The dominant 2026 pattern is one main loop that delegates to sub-agents rather than a single monolithic agent.

  • Side-by-side sessions. Claude Code's desktop "v2" is built around running many agent sessions in parallel, with a live view of what each is doing and faster startup — tooling catching up to how people actually drive agents. (Claude Code, Oct 15)
  • Self-spawning. Agents are becoming "aware of their own capabilities" — e.g. spinning off sub-sessions when a task fans out. (Thariq Shihipar / @trq212)
  • When to fold vs. split. An open design question the field is actively writing about: when a sub-agent earns its keep vs. when it should be folded back into the main loop. (flagged as a gap — article at claude.com/resources/articles not yet captured; source added 2026-10-28)

Agent-scale engineering

The clearest structural signal of the year: once most commits are agent-generated, human-scale infrastructure breaks.

  • Anthropic rebuilt its own source control after merge volume hit 30k+/day — dev infra designed for humans bottlenecked on agent throughput. A preview of problems any team scaling agentic coding will hit. (Anthropic Engineering, Oct 14)

Embodied & tool-using agents

  • Code-your-own-simulation. Gemini 4 Argon watched a cornhole video, mapped the physics, wrote its own simulation, and taught Apptronik's Apollo humanoid to sink throws — video-grounded, self-authored tooling rather than a fixed skill. (DeepMind, Oct 14)
  • Voice agents. Grok Voice 3.0 (sub-1s latency, #1 Speech-to-Speech) is already powering customer-support voice in production, pushing real-time spoken agents mainstream. (xAI, Oct 15)

Open questions to watch

  • Sub-agent orchestration: fold vs. split, context handoff, cost of parallel sessions.
  • Agent memory across sessions (durable state, not just context window).
  • Infra for agent-scale output: review, merge, CI, and attribution when humans aren't the bottleneck.
